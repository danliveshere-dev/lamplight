Small building blocks for real HTML. These examples use the core stylesheet; add behaviour only when it serves the task.

Typography and reading

Notes from the garden

Clear type and a comfortable measure make a longer thought easier to return to. A panel can give that thought a place without competing with it.

A short note can sit alongside the reading.

<article class="l-panel">
  <h3>Notes from the garden</h3>
  <div class="l-reading">...</div>
</article>

Use headings in document order and choose a reading measure that suits the content. Panel depth is a visual choice; reserve focal treatment for one thing that needs emphasis.

Leads, threads and knots

These quiet marks establish hierarchy and continuity. They are decoration around meaningful text, not controls or a substitute for state labels.

A lead for a region

A short rule gives the section a point of entry.

Plan the next visit
Ready to begin
Record what changed
<div class="l-lead-line">...</div>
<div class="l-thread">
  <div class="l-knot" data-kind="lead">Plan the next visit</div>
  <div class="l-knot" data-state="live">Ready to begin</div>
</div>

The lead line is a decorative two-pixel rule at 75% width, coloured by --l-tone (accent by default). A thread has a 9px head and 2px line; knots are 6px. A lead knot uses semibold text without an extra dot. Live is a visual state, so also name the state in text. These marks are still; Ledger removes halos and Folio renders in black. Keep the underlying reading order meaningful.

Chapter openers and navigation

A serif Roman numeral gives each chapter a clear point of entry. The title and numeral share a baseline, with a short argument aligned to the title.

A useful place to begin

Leave one clear step for the next visit.

<header class="l-chapter">
  <span class="l-chapter-number" aria-hidden="true">IV</span>
  <h3 class="l-chapter-title">A useful place to begin</h3>
  <p class="l-chapter-argument">Leave one clear step for the next visit.</p>
</header>

Choose a heading level that fits the document. The numeral is decorative; the heading provides the chapter's name.

Surfaces and print

Put l-room around a region and choose data-mode="night" or "day", and data-surface="study", "ledger" or "folio". The choices are independent. Folio prepares content for print. Print layouts need current form values as text, hidden controls, and content that fits the page. See the working examples.
